Accuracy against FEM and validation evidence

All three methods are compared with the same FEM reference database. Hcr is interval-censored: 47 curves are bracketed by Hs < Hcr ≤ Hf and 16 curves are stable through 10 m (Hcr > 10 m). Interval coverage, interval-distance MAE and right-censoring violations are the primary Hcr criteria; R², RMSE, MAE and MAPE use the interval midpoint of the 47 bounded curves and are secondary diagnostics. FS metrics use the 372 stable FEM points (failed states are excluded, never set to FS = 0).
Important: this tab is a fit-to-database comparison. The equations were calibrated on all 63 curves, and XGBoost / xDeepFM predictions on their training curves are in-sample, so ML values here are optimistic. Use “Validation evidence” below for out-of-fold results.

Proposed engineering equations

Hcr — Method A

Ĥcr = c0 + c1·G + c2·θ² + c3·Wb + c4·θ·Wb
= 15.5248373 − 1.52461686·G − 0.00063835·θ² − 7.70343505·Wb + 0.03235125·θ·Wb

G = 1, 2, 3 (Soil Group) · θ = slope angle (°)
Wb = 0 for WET, 1 for DRY or RAPID
GUI safeguard: for Wb = 1 the angle terms form a parabola with vertex at θ* = −c4/(2c2) = 25.3°; below θ* the GUI holds θ at θ* (clamp) and shows a warning for θ < 30°. The clamp changes Ĥcr by at most 0.02 m (G1, 20°: 6.69 → 6.71 m).

Calibrated directly from the 63 FEM curves with an interval-censored Gaussian regression:
bounded curve: P(Hs < Hcr ≤ Hf) = Φ((Hf − μ)/σ) − Φ((Hs − μ)/σ)
stable through 10 m: P(Hcr > 10) = 1 − Φ((10 − μ)/σ), μ = Ĥcr
The interval midpoint is not a fitting target and 10 m is not an exact Hcr.
Interval-distance MAE is an evaluation metric, not the fitting objective.
Any prediction above 10 m is reported as “Hcr > 10 m” (beyond the tested range).

FS — Softplus-7

z = b0 + b1·ln(H) + b2·T + b3·G + b4·W + b5·ln(H)·G + b6·T²
FS = 1 + ln(1 + exp(z)), T = tan(θ)

b0 = 6.808680 · b1 = −2.971959
b2 = −0.571198 · b3 = −1.238220
b4 = −1.881010 · b5 = 0.427553
b6 = 0.052114

W = 0 for WET, 1 for DRY or RAPID
Fitted to the 372 stable FEM rows only; failed / N/A states are not FS = 0 and FS is not interpolated across the failure boundary.

Why DRY and RAPID share one Wb in Method A

DRY and RAPID produced identical observed Hcr intervals for all 21 Soil Group × slope-angle combinations in the FEM database. Their separate Hcr effects are therefore not identifiable at the available 1 m excavation-depth resolution, and they are combined as a single non-WET indicator Wb for Method A. This does not imply that DRY and RAPID produce identical FS responses; they are not forced to be identical in the FS models.

Soil group assignment

Raw3 = −0.56507713·(γs − 17.066667)/2.13124898 + 0.57947262·(es − 1.900333)/0.46761832 + 0.58728129·(Ts − 15.666667)/4.92160769; Score3 = (Raw3 + 2.07394105)/4.15172029. G1 if Score3 < 0.249307; G2 if 0.249307 ≤ Score3 < 0.749307; G3 otherwise. This 7→3 reduction is a site-specific practical classifier built on only three representative soil profiles; it is not evidence that γs, es and Ts are universally dominant or causal slope-stability parameters. Method A and Softplus-7 use G, not the individual soil parameters; the classifier only suggests a group and never changes it without confirmation.

The three methods are intentionally shown independently. Method A and Softplus-7 are FEM-calibrated engineering equations fitted directly to the FEM results; they are not compressed or distilled forms of xDeepFM or XGBoost. Calibrated scope: three soil groups, θ = 20–80°, H = 1–10 m; no independent-site validation has been performed.
Method A, Softplus-7 and the reported validation statistics follow Slope_Hcr_FS_Master_Framework_ResearchReady_Refit.xlsx (630 FEM cases: 372 stable, 258 failed; 63 curves: 47 bounded, 16 right-censored). XGBoost and xDeepFM are independent FEM surrogates; the proposed equations are fitted directly to FEM.
